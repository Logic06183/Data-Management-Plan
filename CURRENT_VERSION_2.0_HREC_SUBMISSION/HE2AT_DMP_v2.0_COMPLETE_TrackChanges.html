1. Executive Summary

Enhanced Executive Summary:

This Data Management Plan outlines procedures for data handling within the HE²AT Center's research activities. This comprehensive Data Management Plan defines enhanced procedures and standards for data acquisition, processing, storage, and sharing within the HE²AT Center Project, incorporating strategic cloud infrastructure migration to improve security, scalability, and operational efficiency.

Key enhancements in Version 2.0 include:

  • Strategic transition from UCT on-premise to WHC-managed cloud infrastructure during Year 5
  • Implementation of AES-256 encryption for all data at rest and in transit
  • 24/7 Security Operations Centre (SOC) monitoring and threat detection
  • Enhanced Data Access Committee (DAC) governance framework
  • Streamlined institutional responsibilities and role definitions
  • Automated backup and disaster recovery systems
  • GPU-accelerated computational resources for complex analyses

2. Scope and Applicability

This Data Management Plan (DMP) applies to all data acquired and produced as part of the activities of the HE²AT Center Project including all associated Pilot projects and collaborative research initiatives conducted under the NIH U54 Cooperation Agreement.

Enhanced scope includes:

  • All health-related data collected under RP1 and RP2 ethics approvals
  • Climate and environmental datasets from institutional partners
  • Socio-economic and geospatial data for population health analyses
  • Remote sensing and satellite data products
  • Derived and synthetic datasets created through HE²AT Center analyses
  • Data shared with external researchers through DAC-approved projects

3. Purpose and Objectives

The purpose of the DMP is to define procedures and standards for the acquisition, transfer, processing, storage, and access to data within the program HE²AT Center Project. This plan ensures data integrity and security whilst facilitating effective data sharing and stakeholder collaboration in accordance with FAIR data principles.

Primary objectives include:

  1. Ensure robust data security and regulatory compliance throughout the cloud migration
  2. Facilitate efficient data sharing while maintaining appropriate access controls
  3. Implement scalable infrastructure to support growing data volumes and computational needs
  4. Maintain data integrity and availability through enhanced backup and recovery systems
  5. Support FAIR data principles for improved research reproducibility and collaboration

While the DMP includes details of access to data by external parties, data sharing outside of the consortium is further supported by the associated Data Sharing Transfer Agreements (DTAs).

5. Background and Context

The Heat and Health in Africa Transdisciplinary Center (HE²AT Center) is a U54 Cooperation agreement with the NIH (2021-2026). The HE²AT Center aspires to become a Center of Excellence in heat-health research, capacity building and engagement, using a population health science approach and applying data science methodologies to improve the health of populations in Africa and beyond.

5.1 Enhanced Cloud Infrastructure Implementation

New Section: The HE²AT Center is implementing a strategic transition from UCT on-premise infrastructure to WHC-managed cloud infrastructure during Year 5, with the goal of complete migration of all sensitive health data to the cloud by the end of Year 5. This transition enhances security, scalability, and operational efficiency whilst maintaining full regulatory compliance.

Strategic benefits include:

  • Enhanced data security through AES-256 encryption and 24/7 SOC monitoring
  • Improved disaster recovery and business continuity capabilities
  • Scalable computational resources to support growing research demands
  • Automated compliance monitoring and reporting
  • Cost-effective long-term infrastructure management

5.2 Research Context and Ethics Framework

Enhanced research framework: The HE²AT Center operates under comprehensive ethics approvals (RP1-220605 and RP2-220606) that encompass all data collection, processing, and sharing activities. The enhanced data management procedures maintain full compliance with these approvals whilst improving technical capabilities.

6. Data Categories and Types

Multiple categories of data will be used across the project broadly divided into three four categories:

6.1 Health-related Data

Enhanced categorization with detailed access controls:

Data TypeDescriptionStorage LocationAccess Level

6.2 Climate and Weather Data

These data include both observational based datasets (weather station observations, satellite proxy observations, and processed/gridded observations). The data will be stored on IBM Research Africa and CSAG/UCT systems, with relevant datasets or subsets also stored on the WHC-managed cloud services to allow for association/linkage with health datasets.

Climate data categories include:

  • Real-time meteorological observations from weather station networks
  • Historical climate datasets spanning multiple decades
  • Climate model outputs and projections
  • Satellite-derived atmospheric and surface measurements
  • Processed and quality-controlled gridded climate products

6.3 Remote sensing data Areal/Geospatial Socio-economic Data

These data represent measures of socio-economic and related conditions such as household economic status, access to services such as water and sanitation, and dwelling type. Data will be stored on CSAG/UCT systems while a copy will be stored on the WHC-managed cloud services.

Socio-economic data types include:

  • Census and demographic data at various geographic scales
  • Household survey data on economic conditions and living standards
  • Infrastructure and service accessibility indicators
  • Urban planning and development datasets
  • Administrative boundary and geographic reference data

6.4 Remote Sensing Data

Data derived from satellite sensors, mainly optical imagery and indicators of physical measures such as land surface temperature, soil moisture estimates, vegetation condition, land use and cover, etc.

Remote sensing data products include:

  • Land surface temperature measurements from thermal sensors
  • Vegetation indices and agricultural monitoring products
  • Land use and land cover classification datasets
  • Soil moisture and hydrological indicators
  • Urban heat island mapping and analysis products

7. Data Management Workflow

Enhanced workflow incorporating cloud infrastructure and automation:

The enhanced data management workflow now includes cloud-based components for improved scalability, security, and disaster recovery capabilities. The transition maintains all existing ethical and regulatory compliance standards while enhancing technical infrastructure and introducing automated quality control processes.

7.1 Data Acquisition and Ingestion

Structured data acquisition process:

  1. Data Source Identification: Systematic cataloguing of all data sources and providers
  2. Ethics and Legal Clearance: Verification of appropriate approvals and agreements
  3. Secure Data Transfer: Encrypted transfer protocols and audit trails
  4. Initial Quality Assessment: Automated data validation and completeness checks
  5. Metadata Documentation: Comprehensive documentation of data provenance and characteristics

7.2 Data Transfer and Storage

Previous text: Once transferred to UCT, if the datasets constitutes personally identifiable data, or if stipulated by the DSA, the dataset will be encrypted for storage using 256-bit AES

New text: Once transferred to UCT or the WHC-managed cloud infrastructure, any data identified as containing Personal Identifiers or specified by the DTA is encrypted for storage using the AES-256 encryption standard for both UCT servers and cloud storage. All data transfers are logged and monitored through automated systems with real-time security scanning.

7.3 Data Processing Pipeline

Five-stage processing pipeline:

  1. Data Validation: Automated quality checks and error detection
  2. Standardisation: Format conversion and variable harmonisation
  3. De-identification: Removal of personal identifiers and implementation of participant IDs
  4. Quality Control: Statistical validation and outlier detection
  5. Documentation: Comprehensive metadata creation and version control

12. Data Sharing and Open Access

12.1 Data Analysis Platform

Previous text: Data analysis involving the Consortium Shared Data and RP1/RP2 de-identified data will initially be facilitated through the CSAG/UCT Jupyter Hub platform

New text: Data analysis involving the Consortium Shared Data and RP1/RP2 de-identified data will initially be facilitated through the CSAG/UCT Jupyter Hub platform and will transition to being supported by cloud-based computational resources with GPU acceleration for complex analyses, improved processing times, and enhanced analytical capabilities

12.2 Data Access Committee Governance

Enhanced DAC framework: All projects, regardless of whether they involve internal HE²AT Center Consortium members or external researchers, must go through the DAC for data access approval. This ensures equitable data sharing and maintains comprehensive tracking of all projects using the data.

DAC review process includes:

  1. Application Submission: Detailed research proposal and data requirements
  2. Ethics Review: Verification of appropriate ethics approvals
  3. Technical Assessment: Evaluation of data requirements and usage plans
  4. Legal Review: DTA negotiation and execution
  5. Access Provisioning: Secure data delivery and access configuration
  6. Ongoing Monitoring: Usage tracking and compliance verification

12.3 Open Access and Public Data Sharing

FAIR data principles implementation:

  • Findable: Comprehensive metadata and cataloguing systems
  • Accessible: Standardised access protocols and authentication systems
  • Interoperable: Common data formats and vocabulary standards
  • Reusable: Clear licensing and usage guidelines

18. Assessment and Revision

Enhanced review process with expanded stakeholder involvement: Additional legal and compliance review will be conducted by UCT legal counsel or WHC legal representative to ensure all changes maintain legal compliance and align with institutional obligations before final approval.

The Data Management Plan will be periodically assessed by the DMAC co-PIs in consultation with WHC leadership including RP1 and RP2 leads. This will take place at least every 6 months quarterly during the transition period, returning to bi-annual reviews post-implementation.

18.1 Review Criteria and Metrics

Comprehensive assessment framework:

  • Technical Performance: System uptime, processing speed, storage capacity
  • Security Metrics: Incident frequency, response times, compliance scores
  • User Satisfaction: Researcher feedback, ease of use, support quality
  • Compliance Status: Regulatory adherence, audit results, corrective actions
  • Cost Effectiveness: Resource utilisation, cost per transaction, ROI analysis

19. Implementation Timeline

Detailed 5-Phase Cloud Migration Strategy:

Phase 1: Planning and Assessment (Months 1-3)

  • Infrastructure evaluation and requirements analysis
  • Security assessment and compliance review
  • Stakeholder consultation and communication plan
  • Technical architecture design and validation
  • Risk assessment and mitigation planning

Phase 2: Pilot Implementation (Months 4-6)

  • Limited dataset migration for testing and validation
  • Security testing and penetration testing
  • User training and feedback collection
  • Performance benchmarking and optimisation
  • Process refinement and documentation

Phase 3: Gradual Migration (Months 7-12)

  • Systematic transfer of datasets with parallel operations
  • Continuous monitoring and quality assurance
  • User support and issue resolution
  • Integration testing and validation
  • Performance monitoring and optimisation

Phase 4: Full Migration (Months 13-18)

  • Complete transition of all sensitive health data to cloud infrastructure
  • Decommissioning of legacy systems
  • Final security validation and compliance certification
  • Comprehensive user training and documentation
  • Operational procedures finalisation

Phase 5: Optimisation (Months 19-24)

  • Performance tuning and full cloud capability utilisation
  • Advanced analytics and reporting implementation
  • Continuous improvement and innovation
  • Long-term planning and strategic development
  • Knowledge transfer and capacity building

Each phase includes comprehensive risk mitigation strategies, stakeholder communication protocols, and rollback procedures to ensure seamless transition while maintaining data integrity and regulatory compliance.
