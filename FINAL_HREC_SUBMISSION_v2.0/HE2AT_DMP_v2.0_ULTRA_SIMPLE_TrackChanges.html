2. Background and Overview

Basic background mentioning HE²AT Center activities and data sharing arrangements

Background to the HE²AT Center: The Heat and Health in Africa Transdisciplinary Center (HE²AT Center) operates under a U54 Cooperation agreement with the National Institutes of Health (2021-2026), serving as a flagship program within the NIH's Data Science for Health Discovery and Innovation in Africa (DS-I Africa) initiative. The Center employs advanced data science methodologies and population health science approaches to investigate the complex relationships between climate variability, extreme heat exposure, and health outcomes across diverse African populations and environments. Scope of Data Management Plan: This comprehensive framework governs all aspects of data acquisition, processing, storage, analysis, and sharing within the HE²AT Center's research ecosystem. The plan addresses: - Multi-institutional data collaboration and governance - Cloud-based infrastructure transition and management - Enhanced regulatory compliance and participant protection - Sophisticated data science methodologies and analytical workflows - Integration with broader DS-I Africa Open Data Science Platform initiatives Purpose: To establish a robust, scalable, and compliant data governance framework that: - Ensures the highest standards of participant privacy and data protection - Facilitates advanced climate-health research through sophisticated data integration - Supports multi-institutional collaboration across diverse regulatory environments - Provides transparent and accountable data access and sharing procedures - Maintains alignment with international best practices for health data management Overview of Data Management Workflow: The HE²AT Center implements a sophisticated five-stage data management workflow: 1. Data Acquisition: Secure collection and transfer of health, climate, and socio-economic data 2. Pre-Processing: Quality assurance, validation, and standardization procedures 3. Harmonisation: Variable mapping, codebook alignment, and format consistency 4. De-identification: Comprehensive removal of direct and indirect identifiers 5. Data Sharing: Governed access through Data Access Committee review and approval

3. Data Types

Basic data categories: 1. Health related data 2. Climate/weather data 3. Remote sensing data 4. Areal/Geospatial socio-economic data 5. Social media data (pilot)

Data Classification Framework: 3.1 Health-related data The HE²AT Center manages health data through a sophisticated security classification system: Original Study Data (Highest Security Classification): - Individual-level health records with full identifiers - Longitudinal patient data and clinical measurements - Detailed demographic and socio-economic information - Restricted to Core Data Team members with security clearance Consortium-shared data (Intermediate Security Classification): - Partially de-identified health datasets for collaborative analysis - Aggregated health indicators and population-level metrics - Geographic health data at district or regional levels - Access restricted to verified consortium members RP1/RP2 De-identified data (Public Research Use): - Fully de-identified datasets suitable for broader research community - Aggregate health indicators without individual-level detail - Public health surveillance data and population statistics - Available through Data Access Committee review process 3.2 Climate/weather data Comprehensive meteorological datasets including: - High-resolution temperature and humidity measurements - Precipitation patterns and extreme weather events - Heat index calculations and thermal stress indicators - Satellite-derived climate data and reanalysis products - Integration with global climate monitoring networks 3.3 Areal/geospatial socio-economic data Multi-scale socio-economic indicators including: - Census data and demographic characteristics - Economic indicators and development metrics - Infrastructure and service availability data - Land use patterns and urbanization indicators - Social vulnerability indices and community characteristics

8. De-identification

Basic de-identification procedures

Comprehensive De-identification Framework: Direct Identifier Removal: - Names, addresses, contact information, and identification numbers - Dates of birth, admission, discharge, and death (converted to age ranges or offset dates) - Geographic identifiers smaller than county/district level - Biometric identifiers and unique physical characteristics - Photographic images and video recordings Indirect Identifier Management: - Rare disease or condition indicators requiring special handling - Unusual demographic combinations that could enable re-identification - Geographic and temporal clustering that might reveal identity - Professional or occupational information in small communities - Family relationship indicators and household composition data Statistical Disclosure Control: - K-anonymity implementation ensuring minimum group sizes - L-diversity maintenance across sensitive attributes - T-closeness verification for attribute distribution protection - Differential privacy application for aggregate statistics - Re-identification risk assessment and monitoring Expert Review Process: - Independent review by privacy and statistical disclosure specialists - Community representative consultation for cultural sensitivity - Legal review for regulatory compliance verification - Ongoing monitoring and re-assessment of de-identification effectiveness - Documentation of all de-identification decisions and procedures
