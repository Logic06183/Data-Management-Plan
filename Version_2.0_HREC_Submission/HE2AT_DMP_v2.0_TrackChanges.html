Scope

This Data Management Plan (DMP) applies to all data acquired and produced as part of the activities of the HE²AT Center Project including Pilot projects.

Purpose

The purpose of the DMP is to define procedures and standards for the acquisition, transfer, processing, storage, and access to data within the program HE²AT Center Project. This plan ensures data integrity and security whilst facilitating effective data sharing and stakeholder collaboration. While the DMP includes details of access to data by external parties, data sharing outside of the consortium is further supported by the associated Data Sharing Transfer Agreements.

Background

The HEat and HEalth in Africa Transdisciplinary Center (HE²AT Center), is a U54 Cooperation agreement with the NIH (2021-2026). The HE²AT Center aspires to become a Center of Excellence in heat-health research, capacity building and engagement, using a population health science approach and applying data science methodologies to improve the health of populations in Africa and beyond.

Enhanced Cloud Infrastructure Implementation

New Section: The HE²AT Center is implementing a strategic transition from UCT on-premise infrastructure to WHC-managed cloud infrastructure during Year 5, with the goal of complete migration of all sensitive health data to the cloud by the end of Year 5. This transition enhances security, scalability, and operational efficiency whilst maintaining full regulatory compliance.

Key enhancements include:

  • AES-256 encryption for all data at rest and in transit
  • 24/7 Security Operations Centre (SOC) monitoring
  • Automated backup and disaster recovery systems
  • Cloud-based computational resources with GPU acceleration
  • Enhanced compliance monitoring and automated reporting

Data Categories

Multiple categories of data will be used across the project broadly divided into three four categories:

1. Health related data

Enhanced categorization of health data:

  • Original Study Data: Raw, unprocessed data held under strong access control on servers hosted at UCT on UCT servers, transitioning to WHC-managed cloud infrastructure with AES-256 encryption from Year 5
  • Consortium Shared Data: Data processed, harmonised, and integrated for HE²AT Center Consortium analysis
  • RP1/RP2 De-identified Data: Further processed and de-identified data available to external researchers through Data Access Committee approval
  • Inferential Data: Aggregated or synthetic data derived from analyses, available for open access

2. Climate/weather data

These data include both observational based datasets (weather station observations, satellite proxy observations, and processed/gridded observations). The data will be stored on IBM Research Africa and CSAG/UCT systems, with relevant datasets or subsets also stored on the WHC-managed cloud services to allow for association/linkage with health datasets.

3. Remote sensing data Areal/geospatial socio-economic data

These data represent measures of socio-economic and related conditions such as household economic status, access to services such as water and sanitation, and dwelling type. Data will be stored on CSAG/UCT systems while a copy will be stored on the WHC-managed cloud services.

4. Remote sensing data

Data derived from satellite sensors, mainly optical imagery and indicators of physical measures such as land surface temperature, soil moisture estimates, vegetation condition, land use and cover, etc.

Data Management Workflow

Updated workflow incorporating cloud infrastructure:

The enhanced data management workflow now includes cloud-based components for improved scalability, security, and disaster recovery capabilities. The transition maintains all existing ethical and regulatory compliance standards while enhancing technical infrastructure.

Data Transfer and Storage

Previous text: Once transferred to UCT, if the datasets constitutes personally identifiable data, or if stipulated by the DSA, the dataset will be encrypted for storage using 256-bit AES

New text: Once transferred to UCT or the WHC-managed cloud infrastructure, any data identified as containing Personal Identifiers or specified by the DTA is encrypted for storage using the AES-256 encryption standard for both UCT servers and cloud storage

Enhanced Security Measures

New Section: From Year 5, enhanced cloud security measures include:

  • AES-256 Encryption: All data at rest and in transit
  • 24/7 SOC Monitoring: Continuous security monitoring and threat detection
  • Role-based Access Controls: Multi-factor authentication and granular permissions
  • Automated Backup Systems: Enhanced disaster recovery capabilities
  • Compliance Monitoring: Automated reporting for regulatory compliance

Data Management, Documentation and Curation

Updated text: Data will be managed by the DMAC team and primary data management will take place on the CSAG/UCT data platform the CSAG/UCT data platform, transitioning to WHC-managed cloud infrastructure with enhanced security and automated backup systems.

Database Population: Database population utilises cloud-native database services, providing automated backup and recovery systems with enhanced data integrity and disaster recovery capabilities.

Data Security and Confidentiality

Network Security

Previous text: The CSAG compute infrastructure benefits from UCT's comprehensive security policies

New text: The CSAG compute infrastructure benefits from UCT's comprehensive security policies, and from Year 5, from WHC-managed cloud security architecture with 24/7 Security Operations Centre (SOC) monitoring and advanced threat detection systems for improved security posture and compliance

Authentication and Authorization

Previous text: Beyond UCT's broader security measures, the CSAG/UCT platform employs additional authentication and authorisation protocols

New text: Beyond UCT's broader security measures, the CSAG/UCT platform employs additional authentication and authorisation protocols alongside cloud-specific access controls and automated compliance reporting for streamlined governance oversight during the transition period

Data Sharing and Open Access

Data Analysis Platform

Previous text: Data analysis involving the Consortium Shared Data and RP1/RP2 de-identified data will initially be facilitated through the CSAG/UCT Jupyter Hub platform

New text: Data analysis involving the Consortium Shared Data and RP1/RP2 de-identified data will initially be facilitated through the CSAG/UCT Jupyter Hub platform and will transition to being supported by cloud-based computational resources with GPU acceleration for complex analyses, improved processing times, and enhanced analytical capabilities

Data Access Committee

New requirement: All projects, regardless of whether they involve internal HE²AT Center Consortium members or external researchers, must go through the DAC for data access approval. This ensures equitable data sharing and maintains comprehensive tracking of all projects using the data.

POPIA Compliance and Protection of Personal Information

Enhanced compliance framework: All enhanced data management procedures maintain full POPIA compliance:

  • Data stored on secure cloud servers with appropriate cross-border transfer protections
  • Only the research team will have access to participant data through enhanced authentication protocols
  • Data will continue to be anonymized using participant IDs
  • All cloud infrastructure changes enhance existing compliance standards

Roles and Responsibilities

  • Introduction of WHC alongside UCT and IBM as key institutional partners
  • Consolidation of platform management oversight across UCT, IBM, and WHC during Year 5
  • Generalization of team roles for improved flexibility and continuity
  • Enhanced focus on institutional capabilities rather than individual responsibilities

Assessment and Revision

Enhanced review process: Additional legal and compliance review will be conducted by UCT legal counsel or WHC legal representative to ensure all changes maintain legal compliance and align with institutional obligations before final approval.

The Data Management Plan will be periodically assessed by the DMAC co-PIs in consultation with WHC leadership including RP1 and RP2 leads. This will take place at least every 6 months quarterly during the transition period, returning to bi-annual reviews post-implementation.

Implementation Timeline

New Section - Structured 5-Phase Cloud Migration:

  1. Phase 1: Planning and Assessment - Infrastructure evaluation and migration strategy finalization
  2. Phase 2: Pilot Implementation - Limited dataset migration for testing and validation
  3. Phase 3: Gradual Migration - Systematic transfer of datasets with parallel operations
  4. Phase 4: Full Migration - Complete transition of all sensitive health data to cloud infrastructure
  5. Phase 5: Optimization - Performance tuning and full cloud capability utilization

Each phase includes comprehensive risk mitigation strategies, stakeholder communication protocols, and rollback procedures to ensure seamless transition while maintaining data integrity and regulatory compliance.

POPIA Compliance Statement

Enhanced Compliance Declaration: According to the rules of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (as amended), all data management enhancements in Version 2.0 maintain or exceed existing compliance standards. Data collected through cloud systems will be stored on secure servers with appropriate encryption and access controls. Where cloud infrastructure may be hosted outside of South Africa, appropriate cross-border data transfer protections and compliance measures are implemented to ensure continued POPIA compliance.
