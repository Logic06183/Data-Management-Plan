1. Stakeholders and Target Audience

This Data Management Plan (DMP) serves multiple stakeholder groups within the HE²AT Center ecosystem and the broader research community:

Internal Stakeholders

  • HE²AT Center Consortium: All funded partners conducting research using shared datasets, adhering to data acquisition, transfer, processing, storage, and access guidelines
  • Data Management and Analysis Core (DMAC): Responsible for implementing and overseeing all data management activities
  • Research Project Teams: RP1 and RP2 investigators conducting heat-health research
  • HE²AT Center Steering Committee: Provides strategic oversight and approves major data management decisions
  • Data Access Committee (DAC): Reviews data access requests and ensures compliance with ethical and legal standards

External Stakeholders

  • Data Providers: Original study investigators and institutions sharing health data
  • Bona Fide Researchers: External scientists requesting access to de-identified datasets
  • Ethics Review Boards: Institutional committees overseeing research compliance
  • Regulatory Bodies: Agencies ensuring adherence to data protection legislation
  • DS-I Africa Program: Broader NIH initiative for data science capacity building

Target Audience

This DMP is designed for:

  • Research administrators and data managers implementing operational procedures
  • Principal investigators and research teams conducting heat-health studies
  • Ethics committees reviewing data sharing proposals
  • Legal and compliance officers ensuring regulatory adherence
  • External researchers seeking data access
  • Funding agencies and oversight bodies

2. Background and Overview of Data Management Plan

2.1. Background to the HE²AT Center

The Heat and Health in Africa Transdisciplinary Center (HE²AT Center) is a U54 Cooperation agreement with the NIH (2021-2026). The HE²AT Center aspires to become a Center of Excellence in heat-health research, capacity building and engagement, using a population health science approach and applying data science methodologies to improve the health of populations in Africa and beyond.

2.2. Scope of Data Management Plan
Original v1.0 Scope:
This Data Management Plan (DMP) applies to all data acquired and produced as part of the activities of the HE²AT Center including Pilot projects.

This comprehensive Data Management Plan (DMP) applies to all data acquired, processed, stored, and shared as part of the HE²AT Center Project activities, including:

  • Research Project 1 (RP1): Individual participant data meta-analysis
  • Research Project 2 (RP2): Urban heat and health investigations
  • Pilot projects and ancillary studies
  • Data Management and Analysis Core (DMAC) activities
  • Training and Engagement Core (TEC) data components

2.3. Purpose
Original v1.0 Purpose:
The purpose of the DMP is to define procedures and standards for the acquisition, transfer, processing, storage, and access to data within the program. While the DMP includes details of access to data by external parties, data sharing outside of the consortium is further supported by the associated Data Sharing Agreements.

This DMP establishes comprehensive frameworks for:

  • Data acquisition through legally compliant transfer agreements
  • Secure data processing and harmonisation procedures
  • Cloud-based storage and infrastructure management
  • Controlled data access through the Data Access Committee
  • POPIA-compliant personal information protection
  • FAIR principles implementation for data discoverability
  • Governance structures ensuring ethical and legal compliance

2.4. Overview of the Data Management Workflow

The HE²AT Center implements a comprehensive data management workflow encompassing:

  1. Data Acquisition: Legal agreements and ethics approvals
  2. Pre-Processing: Quality control and validation procedures
  3. Harmonisation: Variable standardisation and common codebook alignment
  4. De-identification: Privacy protection through approved methodologies
  5. Integration: Climate, health, and socio-economic data integration
  6. Access Control: DAC-mediated sharing with bona fide researchers
  7. Analysis Platforms: Secure computational environments
  8. Data Retention: Long-term storage and archival procedures

3. Data Types

Original v1.0 Structure:
Multiple categories of data will be used across the project broadly divided into three categories: 1. Health related data, 2. Climate/weather data, 3. Remote sensing data, 4. Areal/Geospatial socio-economic data, 5. Social media data

The HE²AT Center manages diverse data types across three primary categories, each with specific handling requirements and compliance considerations:

3.1. Health-related Data

Health-related data forms the core of HE²AT Center research activities and includes:

Individual Participant Data (IPD)

  • Source: Previous cohort studies and clinical trials
  • Content: Medical records, health events, demographic information
  • Geographic Focus: Sub-Saharan African populations
  • Temporal Coverage: Studies conducted between 2000-2020
  • Access Mechanisms: Direct PI engagement, data sharing platforms (WWARN, BMGFKi)

RP1 Health Data

  • Focus: Pregnant women and maternal-child health outcomes
  • Variables: Preterm birth, pre-eclampsia, neonatal admissions
  • Population Size: Large studies (n>1000)
  • Study Design: Longitudinal cohorts and randomised trials

RP2 Health Data

  • Focus: Urban health outcomes in Johannesburg and Abidjan
  • Geographic Variables: Clinic locations and spatial identifiers
  • Integration Requirements: Linkage with socio-economic and climate data

Compliance Framework

Personal Data Classification: Health data is classified as personal data under POPIA, requiring specific protective measures:

  • Data Transfer Agreements with all providers
  • Ethics committee approvals and notifications
  • AES-256 encryption for storage and transfer
  • Access control through authentication systems
  • De-identification procedures before analysis

3.2. Climate/Weather Data

Climate and weather datasets support environmental exposure assessment:

Observational Data

  • Weather Stations: Ground-based temperature, humidity, precipitation
  • Satellite Observations: Land surface temperature, atmospheric conditions
  • Gridded Products: Processed observational datasets

Model-based Data

  • Reanalysis: ERA5, ERA5-Land, WFDE5 datasets
  • Forecasts: ECMWF operational and seasonal predictions
  • Climate Projections: CORDEX Africa, CP4-A high-resolution simulations

Data Sources and Access

  • Open Repositories: Copernicus Climate Data Store, Earth System Grid Federation
  • Commercial Sources: IBM Weather Company data through PAIRS platform
  • Storage: CSAG/UCT servers with metadata indexing
  • Licensing: Open data policies with citation requirements

3.3. Areal/Geospatial Socio-economic Data

Socio-economic datasets provide contextual variables for vulnerability assessment:

Census and Survey Data

  • South African Census: Household characteristics, service access, dwelling types
  • GCRO Quality of Life Surveys: Gauteng City-Region socio-economic indicators
  • General Household Surveys: Living circumstances and service access

Remote Sensing and Geospatial Data

  • Satellite Imagery: Sentinel-2 for urban form analysis
  • Elevation Data: SRTM 30m resolution
  • Population Data: SEDAC Gridded Population of the World
  • Land Surface Temperature: MODIS datasets

Privacy and Access Considerations

  • Aggregated Data: Census data aggregated to small areas (non-personally identifiable)
  • Open Access: Most remote sensing data available through open policies
  • Restricted Access: Some datasets require institutional agreements

The HE²AT Center implements a comprehensive data processing pipeline ensuring quality, consistency, and integration across diverse health datasets:

Transformation Procedures

  • Unit Conversion: Standardisation to common measurement units

  • Temporal Alignment: Date format standardisation and period definitions
  • Geographic Coding: Location variable transformation and aggregation

5.3. Mapping Validation

Quality Assurance Process

  • Expert Review: Clinical and methodological validation
  • Cross-Study Comparison: Variable distribution and range checks
  • Pilot Testing: Small-scale implementation and error detection
  • Iterative Refinement: Mapping improvement based on validation results

5.4. Database Population

Integration Architecture

  • Version Control: Tracking of data updates and modifications
  • Audit Trail: Complete documentation of processing steps
  • Backup Procedures: Regular data backup and recovery protocols

5.5. RP1/RP2 De-Identified Dataset Creation

De-identification Protocol

  • Safe Harbor Method: Removal of 18 categories of personal identifiers

8. De-identification

Original v1.0 De-identification:
De-identification may be implemented in order to minimize the potential for individuals to be identified either by geospatial information or other personal information.

The HE²AT Center implements rigorous de-identification procedures ensuring participant privacy while maintaining analytical utility:

8.1. Safe Harbour and/or Expert Determination

Safe Harbour Method

Following US Department of Health and Human Services guidelines, the following 18 categories of personal identifiers are systematically removed:

  • Names and initials
  • Geographic subdivisions smaller than state level
  • Dates directly related to individuals (birth, death, admission)
  • Telephone and fax numbers
  • Email addresses and web URLs
  • Social security and medical record numbers
  • Health plan beneficiary numbers
  • Account and certificate numbers
  • Vehicle identifiers and license plate numbers
  • Device identifiers and serial numbers
  • Internet protocol addresses
  • Biometric identifiers
  • Full-face photographs
  • Any other unique identifying characteristics

Expert Determination Process

  • Expert Panel: Statisticians, epidemiologists, and privacy specialists
  • Risk Assessment: Quantitative evaluation of re-identification probability
  • Utility Preservation: Balancing privacy protection with research value
  • Documentation: Formal expert determination reports

8.2. Geographic Aggregation

Spatial De-identification Strategy

  • RP1 Geographic Handling: Aggregation to administrative regions (provinces, districts)
  • RP2 Urban Analysis: Census small areas or wards (2-5 km spatial scale)
  • Record Density Consideration: Minimum threshold of records per geographic unit
  • Population-based Aggregation: Ensuring adequate population coverage for anonymity

Administrative Boundary Selection

  • South African Context: Municipal wards and sub-districts
  • International Studies: Equivalent administrative units
  • Urban vs Rural: Appropriate aggregation for population density
  • Analytical Requirements: Maintaining sufficient spatial resolution for research

8.3. Location Jittering

Coordinate Perturbation Methods

  • Random Displacement: Systematic random offsetting of coordinates
  • Distance Parameters: 10-50 km displacement depending on analysis requirements
  • Direction Randomisation: Random azimuth for coordinate shifting
  • Boundary Constraints: Ensuring jittered points remain within appropriate regions

Preservation of Analytical Utility

  • Climate Zone Maintenance: Ensuring environmental exposure validity
  • Urban-Rural Classification: Preserving settlement type characteristics
  • Elevation Considerations: Maintaining topographic relevance
  • Distance Relationships: Preserving relative spatial patterns

8.4. Expert Review and Risk Assessment

Multi-institutional Expert Panel

  • UCT Representatives: Statistical and epidemiological expertise
  • IBM Technical Specialists: Data science and privacy technology
  • NIH Oversight: Federal privacy and ethics standards
  • External Reviewers: Independent privacy and bioethics experts

Risk Assessment Framework

  • Quantitative Metrics: K-anonymity, L-diversity, T-closeness measures
  • Attack Scenarios: Evaluation of potential re-identification methods
  • Utility Metrics: Assessment of analytical validity post-de-identification
  • Ongoing Monitoring: Regular review of de-identification effectiveness

Documentation and Validation

  • De-identification Reports: Comprehensive documentation of procedures
  • Expert Certifications: Formal approval of de-identification adequacy
  • Audit Trails: Complete tracking of de-identification steps
  • Quality Assurance: Independent verification of de-identification implementation
