Scope Definition

Scope This Data Management Plan (DMP) applies to all data acquired and produced as part of the activities of the HE^2^AT Center including Pilot projects. Stakeholders and target audience This Data Management Plan serves multiple stakeholder groups within the HE²AT Center ecosystem: Primary Stakeholders: - HE²AT Center Core Teams (DMAC, TEC, Admin Core) - Research Project Teams (RP1, RP2) - Institutional Partners (UCT, Wits, IBM) - Data Access Committee (DAC) members - Ethics Review Committees (HREC) Secondary Stakeholders: - External Researchers requesting data access - NIH DS-I Africa Programme coordinators - International collaborators - Regulatory compliance officers Target Audience for Implementation: - Data Management Team members - IT infrastructure administrators - Research coordinators - Ethics committee reviewers - Legal and compliance personnel Scope of Data Management Plan This comprehensive Data Management Plan (DMP) applies to all data acquired, processed, stored, and shared as part of the HE²AT Center activities, including: - Research Projects 1 and 2 (RP1, RP2) - All pilot projects and sub-studies - Consortium-wide data sharing initiatives - Cloud migration and infrastructure transition - Multi-institutional data governance frameworks - International data transfer and storage protocols

Purpose and Objectives

Purpose The purpose of the DMP is to define procedures and standards for the acquisition, transfer, processing, storage, and access to data within the program. While the DMP includes details of access to data by external parties, data sharing outside of the consortium is further supported by the associated Data Sharing Agreements. Purpose This comprehensive Data Management Plan serves as the foundational governance framework for the HE²AT Center's transition to cloud-based infrastructure and sophisticated multi-institutional data management. The DMP establishes: 1. **Comprehensive Data Governance**: Detailed procedures for acquisition, transfer, processing, storage, and sharing of sensitive health data across international boundaries 2. **Cloud Infrastructure Framework**: Technical specifications and security protocols for WHC cloud-based data storage and processing systems 3. **Multi-institutional Collaboration**: Governance structures enabling secure data sharing between UCT, Wits, IBM, and international partners 4. **Ethics and Compliance Integration**: Robust framework ensuring POPIA compliance and international data protection standards 5. **Data Access Committee Operations**: Formal governance structure for evaluating and approving data access requests 6. **Risk Management and Security**: Advanced security protocols including AES-256 encryption, 24/7 SOC monitoring, and incident response procedures 7. **Quality Assurance Framework**: Systematic approaches to data validation, harmonization, and documentation 8. **Sustainability and Scalability**: Infrastructure designed to support expanding research activities and growing data volumes This DMP supersedes previous versions and establishes the operational framework for sophisticated, compliant, and secure data management supporting the HE²AT Center's research objectives while maintaining the highest standards of participant protection and data security.

Background and Context

Background The HEat and HEalth in Africa Transdisciplinary Center, HE2AT Center, is a U54 Cooperation agreement with the NIH (2021-2026). The HE2AT Center aspires to become a Center of Excellence in heat-health research, capacity building and engagement, using a population health science approach and applying data science methodologies to improve the health of populations in Africa and beyond. The goal of the HE2AT Center is to advance the development of new health knowledge and human capacities through reusing existing data to generate, and then disseminate heat-health knowledge and innovations. Background and overview of Data Management Plan Background to the HE²AT Center The Heat and Health in Africa Transdisciplinary Center (HE²AT Center) represents a transformative U54 Cooperation Agreement with the NIH (2021-2026), designed to establish a world-class Center of Excellence in heat-health research, capacity building, and community engagement. Operating within the broader NIH Data Science Initiative Africa (DS-I Africa) framework, the HE²AT Center employs sophisticated population health science approaches and cutting-edge data science methodologies to advance understanding of climate-health interactions across African populations. The Center's mission encompasses four core objectives: 1. **Advanced Research Excellence**: Conducting innovative research on heat-health relationships using state-of-the-art analytical methodologies 2. **Capacity Building**: Developing next-generation African researchers and data scientists through comprehensive training programs 3. **Knowledge Translation**: Facilitating evidence-to-action pathways for climate-health adaptation strategies 4. **Community Engagement**: Ensuring research outcomes benefit the communities most affected by climate change Multi-institutional Collaboration Framework The HE²AT Center operates through a sophisticated consortium model involving: - **University of Cape Town (UCT)**: Climate Systems Analysis Group providing environmental data expertise - **University of the Witwatersrand (Wits)**: Health research leadership through Wits Reproductive Health and HIV Institute - **IBM Research Africa**: Advanced data science capabilities and cloud infrastructure support - **International Partners**: Collaborating institutions across Africa and globally Research Project Portfolio **Research Project 1 (RP1)**: Large-scale Individual Participant Data (IPD) meta-analysis examining associations between ambient temperature exposure and maternal-child health outcomes across sub-Saharan Africa. This project represents the first application of advanced IPD meta-analytical techniques to climate-health research in Africa. **Research Project 2 (RP2)**: Comprehensive urban heat-health risk assessment focused on Johannesburg (South Africa) and Abidjan (Côte d'Ivoire), integrating diverse data streams including health outcomes, environmental monitoring, socio-economic factors, and urban morphology to develop predictive early warning systems. Cloud Infrastructure Transition Version 2.2 of this DMP specifically addresses the Center's strategic transition from traditional institutional computing infrastructure to advanced cloud-based systems managed by the Wits Health Consortium (WHC). This transition enables: - Enhanced data security through enterprise-grade encryption and monitoring - Improved scalability to accommodate growing data volumes - Advanced analytical capabilities through cloud-native tools - Robust compliance with international data protection standards - Seamless collaboration across institutional boundaries

Data Categories and Classification

Data categories Multiple categories of data will be used across the project broadly divided into three categories: 1. Health related data These data will include data collected from previous cohort studies and trials (e.g. HIV treatment trials and cohort studies involving pregnant women, postpartum women and their infants up to 1 years of age) 2. Climate/weather data These data include both observational based datasets (weather station observations, satellite proxy observations, and processed/gridded observations). Gridded climate data produced from atmospheric re-analysis and climate simulations will form historical gridded climate observations and forecasts. 3. Remote sensing data The focus is on data derived from satellite sensors, mainly optical imagery (e.g. satellite images of urban centers) as well as indicators of physical measures such as land surface temperature, soil moisture estimates, vegetation condition, land use and cover, etc.) for the purposes of estimating environmental quantities and land use/building density etc. 4. Areal/Geospatial socio-economic data These data represent measures of socio-economic and related conditions such as household economic status, access to services such as water and sanitation, dwelling type, etc. 5. Social media data These data represent activity on various social media platforms, initially Twitter. Other data that will be investigated will be the Google Search trends and the Facebook mobility data. Data types Health-related data Summary of data categories The HE²AT Center manages a sophisticated taxonomy of health-related data designed to support comprehensive climate-health research while maintaining strict compliance with POPIA and international data protection standards. Health data is classified into five distinct categories based on processing stage, access requirements, and intended use: 1. **Original Study Data**: Raw datasets from primary research studies 2. **Consortium-shared Data**: Processed datasets approved for consortium-wide access 3. **RP1/RP2 De-identified Data**: Datasets prepared for specific research project applications 4. **Inferential Data**: Derived variables and statistical models 5. **Aggregate Data**: Summary statistics and population-level indicators Original Study Data Original study data encompasses raw datasets acquired from primary research studies conducted by consortium partners or obtained through formal data sharing agreements with external research organizations. These datasets contain the most comprehensive information collected during original research activities and require the highest level of security and access control. Key characteristics: - **Data Sources**: Clinical trials, cohort studies, cross-sectional surveys, and longitudinal health studies - **Geographic Coverage**: Primarily sub-Saharan Africa, with specific focus on South Africa, Côte d'Ivoire, and other DS-I Africa partner countries - **Temporal Coverage**: Historical data from 2000-present, with ongoing data collection activities - **Data Volume**: Varies from small focused studies (n<500) to large multi-site trials (n>10,000) - **Access Restrictions**: Limited to core data team members with specific training and authorization Security and Compliance Requirements: - AES-256 encryption for all data at rest and in transit - Access limited to named individuals with signed POPIA processing agreements - Comprehensive audit logging of all access and processing activities - Regular security assessments and compliance monitoring Consortium-shared data Consortium-shared data represents health datasets that have undergone initial processing, quality control, and de-identification procedures, making them suitable for sharing across authorized HE²AT Center consortium partners. These datasets maintain sufficient detail to support sophisticated analytical activities while reducing identification risks. Processing Requirements: - **Quality Control**: Systematic validation of data integrity and completeness - **Harmonization**: Standardization of variable definitions and coding schemes - **De-identification**: Removal or transformation of direct and indirect identifiers - **Documentation**: Comprehensive metadata and codebook development - **Access Control**: Formal data sharing agreements between consortium partners Data Access Framework: - Consortium partners must execute formal data sharing agreements - Access requires institutional ethics approval for intended research activities - Users must complete POPIA compliance training and sign processing agreements - Regular audit and compliance monitoring of data use activities RP1/RP2 De-identified data RP1/RP2 de-identified datasets are specifically prepared for use in the Center's primary research projects, with de-identification procedures tailored to the analytical requirements of each research question while maintaining POPIA compliance and minimizing re-identification risks. RP1 Datasets (IPD Meta-analysis): - **Scope**: Maternal and child health outcomes from multiple African cohorts - **De-identification**: Geographic aggregation to administrative units, date shifting, identifier removal - **Variables**: Pregnancy outcomes, child health indicators, demographic characteristics, environmental exposures - **Sample Size**: Target >50,000 mother-child pairs across multiple studies RP2 Datasets (Urban Heat-Health): - **Scope**: Health outcomes from Johannesburg and Abidjan urban populations - **De-identification**: Spatial jittering, temporal aggregation, health facility anonymization - **Variables**: Cardiovascular and respiratory outcomes, emergency department visits, mortality data - **Integration**: Combined with urban heat indices, socio-economic indicators, and environmental data Inferential data Inferential data encompasses derived variables, statistical models, and analytical outputs generated through sophisticated data science methodologies applied to primary health datasets. This category supports advanced machine learning applications and predictive modeling activities. Types of Inferential Data: - **Derived Health Indicators**: Composite measures of health status and vulnerability - **Risk Prediction Models**: Machine learning models predicting climate-health outcomes - **Spatial Interpolation**: Modeled health indicators for unmeasured geographic areas - **Temporal Forecasting**: Predicted health outcomes under different climate scenarios - **Vulnerability Indices**: Composite measures integrating health, environmental, and social factors Climate/weather data The HE²AT Center maintains comprehensive climate and weather datasets spanning observational records, reanalysis products, and future projections. These datasets provide the environmental context necessary for understanding heat-health relationships and developing predictive models. Observational Data Sources: - **Weather Station Networks**: Ground-based temperature, humidity, and meteorological measurements - **Satellite Products**: Land surface temperature, atmospheric conditions, and environmental indicators - **Reanalysis Products**: ERA5, WFDE5, and other high-resolution climate datasets - **Forecast Systems**: ECMWF, seasonal prediction models, and early warning systems Data Processing and Management: - Standardized quality control procedures for all climate datasets - Harmonization to common spatial and temporal resolutions - Integration with health data through spatiotemporal matching algorithms - Version control and provenance tracking for all processed datasets Areal/geospatial socio-economic data Socio-economic datasets provide crucial contextual information for understanding vulnerability to climate-health impacts and identifying populations at highest risk. These datasets integrate census information, survey data, and administrative records to characterize social determinants of health. Data Sources and Coverage: - **National Census Data**: Population demographics, housing characteristics, economic indicators - **Household Surveys**: Quality of Life surveys, General Household Surveys, demographic and health surveys - **Administrative Data**: Service delivery indicators, infrastructure access, governance measures - **Spatial Data**: Land use, urban morphology, transportation networks, health facility locations Processing and Integration: - Spatial harmonization to common geographic units (census areas, administrative boundaries) - Temporal alignment with health and climate datasets - Quality control and validation procedures - Privacy protection through spatial aggregation and statistical disclosure control

Data Security and Protection

POPIA compliance and protection of personal information The use of health datasets requires careful consideration of data security and confidentiality. This is strongly guided by the relevant legislation context for each datasets including the specific country legislation around the use of personal/sensitive data and the cross border transfer of such datasets. The Protection of Personal Information Act of South Africa (2013) institutes limitations on the processing of personal information, but also provides the legal basis for the use of personal information for the purpose of scientific research. De-identification POPIA Section 10 prescribes the principle of Minimality which means that only information relevant to the purpose of processing should be processed. Data encryption Primary data (before processing) will be stored in encrypted form using 256bit AES (Advanced Encryption Standard) a standard established by the US NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology). Storage isolation Encrypted data will be stored in an isolated virtual storage server that is only accessible to the private data team. Network firewall and Virtual Private Network CSAG compute infrastructure sits within the UCT intranet and falls under the broader UCT security policies and services. POPIA compliance and protection of personal information The HE²AT Center's approach to personal information protection is grounded in comprehensive compliance with the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) of South Africa, enhanced by international best practices and technical safeguards appropriate for cloud-based infrastructure management. POPIA Compliance Framework: The Center's compliance strategy is built on POPIA's core principles while accommodating the complexities of multi-institutional, international research collaboration: **Accountability (Section 8)**: The HE²AT Center maintains comprehensive documentation of all data processing activities, including detailed records of data acquisitions, processing procedures, access controls, and disposal activities. **Processing Limitation (Section 9)**: All personal information processing is limited to research purposes explicitly described in ethics approvals and data transfer agreements, with regular audits ensuring adherence to stated purposes. **Purpose Specification (Section 13-15)**: Data processing purposes are clearly documented and communicated to data subjects through original study consent processes, with secondary use governed by Section 15(3)(e) provisions for historical, statistical, and research purposes. **Further Processing Limitation (Section 15)**: Secondary processing for heat-health research is conducted under Section 15(3)(e) exemptions, with additional safeguards including ethics committee notifications and enhanced security measures. **Information Quality (Section 16)**: Comprehensive quality control procedures ensure data accuracy, completeness, and relevance for research purposes, with systematic validation and error correction protocols. **Openness (Section 18)**: While direct notification to data subjects is often impractical for secondary research, the Center maintains transparency through public documentation of research activities and data use practices. **Security Safeguards (Section 19)**: Advanced technical and organizational measures protect personal information throughout the data lifecycle, including encryption, access controls, audit logging, and incident response procedures. **Data Subject Participation (Section 23-37)**: Although limited for secondary research, the Center maintains procedures for addressing data subject inquiries and exercising rights where feasible. Cloud Infrastructure Security Considerations: Data collected through the WHC cloud infrastructure may be stored on secure servers hosted outside of South Africa, requiring enhanced POPIA compliance measures: - **Cross-border Transfer Safeguards**: Formal adequacy assessments and contractual protections for international data transfers - **Enhanced Encryption**: AES-256 encryption for all data at rest and in transit - **Jurisdiction Analysis**: Legal assessment of data storage locations and applicable privacy frameworks - **Audit and Monitoring**: Continuous compliance monitoring and regular security assessments - **Incident Response**: Comprehensive procedures for managing potential data breaches or security incidents Governance and compliance Data Governance The HE²AT Center employs a multi-layered data governance framework designed to ensure compliance, security, and research excellence across all consortium activities. Assessment and Revision Framework

Assessment and revision The Data Management Plan will be periodically assessed by the DMAC co-PIs in consultation with the HEAT Steering Committee including RP1 and RP2 leads. This will take place at least every 6 months. The assessment will look at three aspects of the data management plan: 1. Data acquisition processes: Are the data aquisition processes working with respect to developing DSAs, transferring the data, and satisfying ethical reviews 2. Data process: Is the data processing workflow working effectively and resulting in data that is ready for analysis? 3. Data analysis support: Is DMAC providing sufficient support and services to enable the data analysis plans for RP1, RP2, and any pilot projects? Based on the assessment, the Data Management Plan will be updated and changes will be implemented. Revisions to the Data Management Plan will be proposed by the DMAC Co-PIs (see above) and approved by the SC. Assessment and revision of the Data Management Plan The HE²AT Center employs a comprehensive, systematic approach to Data Management Plan assessment and revision designed to ensure continuous improvement, regulatory compliance, and alignment with evolving research objectives. This framework reflects the sophisticated requirements of cloud-based infrastructure and multi-institutional collaboration.
