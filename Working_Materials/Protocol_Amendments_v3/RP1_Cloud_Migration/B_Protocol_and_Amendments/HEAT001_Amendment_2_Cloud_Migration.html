PROTOCOL AMENDMENT #2

Study Title: Individual Participant Data meta-analysis to quantify the impact of high ambient temperatures on maternal and child health in Africa

Protocol number: HEAT001

Date of last version of protocol: Version 1.0 (July 2023)

HREC Reference No: 220605

Amendment Date: 01 October 2025

1. Background and Context

The HEAT001 study aims to conduct an individual participant data meta-analysis to quantify the impact of high ambient temperatures on maternal and child health outcomes across Africa. This research addresses critical knowledge gaps in understanding climate-health relationships affecting vulnerable populations.

This protocol amendment proposes targeted enhancements to the data analysis infrastructure described in Section 5 of the protocol. The modifications involve implementing enhanced cloud infrastructure capabilities alongside existing UCT infrastructure to improve data security, computational efficiency, and research collaboration capabilities while maintaining all existing ethical standards and participant protections.

The key areas requiring modification are:

  1. Integration of cloud-based data management and curation capabilities
  2. Enhancement of data security measures through advanced encryption and monitoring
  3. Implementation of cloud-based network security and virtual private networks
  4. Addition of cloud-based authentication and authorization systems
  5. Improvement of data availability procedures through enhanced cloud infrastructure

3. Justification and Impact

The proposed modifications to Section 5 of the HEAT001 protocol represent a strategic enhancement of the research infrastructure rather than a fundamental change to the study design or ethical framework. These changes are justified by the following considerations:

Enhanced Data Security: The implementation of AES-256 encryption, 24/7 SOC monitoring, and automated threat detection significantly exceeds minimum security requirements and provides additional protection for participant data. This enhancement aligns with international best practices for health data management.

Improved Computational Capabilities: The hybrid cloud infrastructure provides scalable computational resources that will enable more sophisticated analyses and faster processing of large datasets, ultimately improving the quality and timeliness of research outputs.

Regulatory Compliance: All cloud infrastructure implementations maintain full compliance with POPIA requirements, including appropriate safeguards for data that may be hosted outside South Africa. The enhanced security measures exceed regulatory minimum requirements.

Research Collaboration: The improved data sharing capabilities and enhanced access management systems will facilitate secure collaboration with international research partners while maintaining strict access controls and audit capabilities.

Risk Mitigation: The implementation of automated backup systems, redundant storage, and enhanced monitoring reduces risks of data loss and unauthorized access, providing additional protection for participant information.

Implementation Strategy: Upon receiving research ethics committee approval for this protocol amendment, the site will implement the protocol modifications in a phased approach over six months, ensuring continuity of existing operations while gradually introducing enhanced capabilities. A copy of the protocol amendment approval along with any correspondence will be retained in the site regulatory files for this study.

These modifications do not affect participant recruitment, consent procedures, data collection methods, or analysis objectives. The enhanced infrastructure capabilities support the existing research aims while providing improved security and efficiency. All ethical obligations and participant protections remain unchanged and are reinforced through the enhanced security measures.
